# A/B: The Same Board Update, With and Without the Skill

Same prompt, same facts, same model:

> "Write a Q3 board pre-brief. Revenue missed target by 12%. Two decisions
> needed: approve the new enterprise pricing tier, and decide the VP Sales
> search — external or internal promotion."

The only difference between A and B is whether the better-documents skill was
installed.

---

## A — Without the skill

**Q3 Board Update**

As we reflect on the past quarter, our team has worked tirelessly to navigate
an increasingly complex market environment. While we faced some headwinds, we
remain committed to our long-term vision and continue to build momentum across
all areas of the business.

### Background

Meridian was founded on the belief that teams deserve better tools. Over the
past year, we've seen strong interest in our platform, and the market continues
to evolve in ways that present both challenges and opportunities. In today's
fast-paced environment, it's more important than ever to stay agile and
focused.

### Market Context

The B2B SaaS landscape is undergoing significant transformation. Customers are
increasingly focused on value and ROI, and competition is intensifying. It's
not just about growth — it's about sustainable, thoughtful growth that serves
our customers well.

| Segment | Target | Actual |
|---|---|---|
| Enterprise | $1.9M | $1.6M |
| Mid-market | $1.6M | $1.4M |
| Small business | $1.15M | $1.1M |

### What We Accomplished

Our team has made remarkable progress during the quarter:

- Launched new features that customers have responded to positively
- Strengthened our partnerships and expanded our network
- Invested in our culture and continued to grow the team
- Improved the product's robustness and seamless integration capabilities

### Challenges We're Navigating

Like any organization, we face challenges. Revenue came in below our internal
expectations for the quarter, and we're seeing some movement in customer
retention that we're actively addressing. We believe these are temporary
setbacks in an otherwise strong trajectory.

### Looking Ahead

As we move into the coming quarter, we're excited about the opportunities
ahead. We're focusing on:

- How can we improve our growth trajectory?
- Deepening customer relationships
- Exploring new market segments

We would welcome the board's approval and guidance on these matters as we
continue to execute on our strategy.

## B — With the skill

**Meridian — Q3 results and two decisions — 2025-10-15**

**What we need from you.** Two decisions, both by November 1:

1. **Approve the enterprise pricing tier.** The enterprise renewal window
   opens November 15. If the tier isn't live before then, we lose an estimated
   $2.1M in ARR from the 14 accounts up for renewal.
2. **Choose the VP Sales path.** Continue the external search (decision by
   December 15) or promote Dana Whitfield internally (decision by November
   15, start December 1). Both options, with costs and risks, are on the next
   page.

**Q3 results.** Revenue was $4.1M against a $4.65M target (−12%). Logos were
flat; the miss is concentration: two of the top ten accounts downgraded, and
one (Halstead) left. Net revenue retention fell to 91% from 104%.

*Q3 revenue by month vs. target:* July $1.3M / $1.5M · August $1.4M / $1.55M ·
September $1.4M / $1.6M

**What we're doing about it.**

- **Pricing.** The enterprise tier bundles the three features the downgraded
  accounts cited in exit interviews. Legal review is done; it ships the week
  of October 20.
- **Retention.** The top 20 accounts now have named owners and quarterly
  business reviews. First round completes October 31.
- **VP Sales.** The external search has three finalists; the internal option
  is detailed in the appendix.

**Risks we're not solving yet.**

- If the renewal window slips, the $2.1M estimate becomes a floor, not a
  target.
- Dana's promotion closes the external search; we can't run both.
- We have no visibility into Halstead's replacement; they may re-tender in Q1.

*Appendix: VP Sales options detail · Q3 financials · Account-level retention
table*

## What changed, pass by pass

### Pass 1 — Audience and purpose

- **A buries the ask.** "We would welcome the board's thoughts" is not an ask;
  it's a courtesy. It appears in the last paragraph, with no deadline and no
  stakes. A board member who reads the first page learns nothing about what
  they're supposed to do.
- **B leads with the ask, the deadline, and why the deadline matters to
  them.** The $2.1M ARR figure is the board's currency, not the company's
  anxiety.
- **A opens author-centered** ("our team has worked tirelessly"). B opens with
  the shared fact both sides already know: the quarter is over and the number
  is what it is.

### Pass 2 — Structure and sequencing

- **A is in creation order:** background → market → accomplishments →
  challenges → future. The revenue miss sits in "Challenges We're
  Navigating," more than halfway down, softened to "below our internal
  expectations."
- **B is in importance order:** decisions → results → response → risks. The
  bad news is in the first third, stated as a number, not a mood.
- **A is a murder mystery.** B is a briefing.

### Pass 3 — Formatting restraint

- In the formatted version (the one in the browser checker), A is where the
  damage shows: bold *and* italic stacked on "remarkable progress," a
  decorative underline on "below our internal expectations," and three accent
  colors on top of the base text. B uses one emphasis type (bold, for the two
  asks) and nothing else. When everything is emphasized, nothing is.

### Pass 4 — Wayfinding and density

- **A's "How can we improve our growth trajectory?"** is a philosophical
  prompt, not a decision. B's questions are answerable with a yes/no or an
  A/B: approve the tier by November 1? External search or internal promotion?
- **A has no signposts** to what the document is asking for. B's four bold
  section labels are the map.
- **A's table has no caption** — it sits under "Market Context" and says
  nothing about what it shows, so the reader has to interpret the numbers
  cold. B's one table is titled with what it shows, and every number has a
  comparison (vs. target, vs. last quarter).

### Pass 5 — Naming and versioning

- **A is "Q3 Board Update"** — generic, undated, unfindable. Six months from
  now, nobody will know which quarter, which company state, or which decisions
  it covered.
- **B is "Meridian — Q3 results and two decisions — 2025-10-15"** — topic,
  content, date. It tells the recipient what the conversation is for before
  they open it.

### The prose tells

A also trips the AI-tell list: "It's not just about growth — it's about
sustainable, thoughtful growth" (contrast reveal), "robustness and seamless
integration" (placeholder adjectives), and an em-dash per paragraph. B states
its points directly.

## The boardroom layer

Neither A nor B could have known what a board actually does with the document.
That's the reference the skill now carries: see
[`references/boardroom.md`](../references/boardroom.md). The short version:

- The pre-brief is the real document. The meeting is where the board reacts;
  the pre-brief is where they form their view.
- Board members skim for three things: surprises, asks, and numbers that
  moved.
- Boards are biased toward the status quo. You're not trying to jolt them into
  radical change; you're making an evolutionary change look possible and safe.
- The realistic goal is rarely "the board agrees with me." It's usually: the
  board approves the specific ask by the date, the board asks leadership about
  X, or — for a team executing well — the board doesn't intervene at all.
